# IssuerNumber.One Strategy Engagement Plan

## Mandate

### Sponsor problem in the sponsor's words

> IssuerNumber.One is part of the Sneat Work ecosystem. Its purpose is to
> become the relationship-aware issue intelligence platform for organisations
> and public communities.

> Determine the strongest business strategy, product strategy and go-to-market
> strategy. Do not assume the current vision is correct. Challenge assumptions.
> Research alternatives. Recommend pivots where appropriate.

For this first output, the sponsor's objective is understanding: establish a
grounded engagement and an approvable plan without recommendations, a roadmap,
architecture or implementation.

### Desired decision or outcome

After the engagement—not during bootstrap—the sponsor should be able to decide:

1. which customer, problem and market IssuerNumber.One should serve first;
2. whether public research, internal organisation intelligence, both, or
   neither should define the product direction;
3. whether relationship-aware issue intelligence creates meaningful customer
   value and a defensible advantage;
4. how IssuerNumber.One should relate to Sneat Work, the shared Sneat platform
   relationship graph and Moodometer without creating a silo; and
5. which business model and acquisition/distribution approach has the strongest
   evidence, including whether LinkedIn deserves priority.

### Framing test

- **Underlying decision:** Where should IssuerNumber.One play, how could it win,
  and what evidence is required before committing product and go-to-market
  resources?
- **Evidence that the mandate addresses it:** The mandate names the required
  business, product and go-to-market decisions and supplies eight explicit
  hypotheses. The current repositories supply a product concept, a demand-probe
  landing and ecosystem context. They do not yet supply customer, usage,
  competitive or market evidence sufficient to choose a direction.
- **Tested framing:** Treat “relationship-aware issue intelligence,” the two
  proposed operating modes, the issue graph, benchmarking, LinkedIn and every
  Sneat platform relationship-graph integration claim as options or hypotheses.
  Compare them with the
  current scarce-priority product, generic survey/feedback categories, the
  status quo and plausible pivots. Do not presuppose that the two modes belong
  in one product or that IssuerNumber.One should remain a standalone product.
- **Sponsor response required:** **Yes.** Approval of this tested framing starts
  the evidence-gathering engagement. It does not authorize sensitive-data
  access, external outreach, canonical changes or implementation.

## Scope

### In scope

- Establish the current product, evidence, maturity and ecosystem baseline.
- Validate, reject or retain uncertainty for every sponsor hypothesis.
- Investigate customers, buyers, jobs, alternatives, market structure,
  willingness to pay and status-quo behaviour.
- Compare the current scarce-priority product with public research and internal
  organisation-intelligence directions.
- Test whether relationship context improves issue collection,
  interpretation, prioritisation, benchmarking or distribution.
- Evaluate defensibility, cold-start dynamics, trust boundaries, data rights
  and the claimed network effect at a strategy level.
- Evaluate business model, pricing hypotheses and go-to-market channels,
  including LinkedIn, without assuming any channel will work.
- Evaluate what signals each option could contribute to the shared Sneat
  platform relationship graph, which other Sneat products could consume them
  and where a silo or trust violation could arise.
- Determine the evidence needed for later recommendations and smallest-useful
  tests.

### Non-goals

- Recommendations, strategic commitments or pivots in this bootstrap output.
- A roadmap; the framework-required `roadmap.md` exists only as an unpopulated
  template at this stage.
- Product or data architecture, domain-model design, API design or trust-domain
  implementation.
- Product implementation, landing changes, deployment, outreach or private-data
  access.
- Editing or promoting canonical product, ecosystem or framework sources.
- Treating fluent analysis, waitlist interest, survey responses or a single
  interview as market validation.
- Redesigning the AI Consulting Framework.

## Engagement journey

The sponsor's journey, preserving the requested outcome, is:

> We begin with IssuerNumber.One as a proposed relationship-aware issue
> intelligence platform, but assume none of its direction is settled. The
> engagement first establishes what is actually known. It then researches the
> problems, customers, alternatives, modes, graph value and routes to market;
> challenges the evidence; and only afterward recommends the strongest
> business, product and go-to-market strategy or a pivot. If the sponsor does
> nothing after approving the plan, safe public and repository research still
> progresses, while sensitive evidence and outreach remain untouched.

Observable good results across the journey:

1. **Start:** This engagement record exists in an isolated workspace; the
   sponsor and Managing Partner can see the tested framing, evidence limits,
   authority, hypotheses, unknowns and next learning action.
2. **Evidence:** Relevant sources, customer/market evidence, contradictions and
   missing evidence are traceable. No thesis is promoted because it sounds
   attractive.
3. **Other-actor visibility:** Each specialist receives the same engagement
   brief, writes a separate report and can see the evidence needed to challenge
   another role's claim. No specialist edits a shared synthesis concurrently.
4. **Choice:** Strategy options—including the status quo and material pivots—are
   compared against declared criteria, with unresolved dissent retained.
5. **Terminal:** The sponsor receives the promised business, product and
   go-to-market conclusions with confidence, risks, decision asks and smallest
   learning paths; every deliverable is reconciled to evidence or an approved
   deferral.
6. **Close epilogue:** If the sponsor stops, no experiment, provisional finding
   or canonical change is implied to remain active.
7. **Share/replay epilogue:** If the sponsor shares or extends the work, an
   authorized reader can recover the mandate, sources, decisions and next
   trigger without this conversation.

There is no product e2e surface for the consulting journey. End-to-end
completion is verified through the source-linked deliverable ledger, immutable
checkpoints and the independent challenge/editorial gates.

## Source-linked work plan

The work plan follows the journey above. No recommendation, roadmap or
architecture task begins before its evidence dependency is met.

1. **Establish the current evidence and demand baseline.** Inventory current
   repository behaviour/maturity, Backstage sources, contradictions, public
   availability and any sponsor-supplied aggregate demand evidence. Record
   searches that find nothing.
   - **Sources:** ecosystem context table and highest-leverage action above.
   - **Output:** `research/current-state-and-demand-evidence.md`.
   - **Verifies:** Facts are linked and freshness-limited; source claims are
     separated from inference; no raw personal data is stored; stale
     `sneat.team`/Sneat Work and hosting claims are explicitly reconciled.

2. **Research customers, buyers, jobs and category alternatives.** Compare the
   status quo and relevant feedback, employee-listening, survey, polling,
   research, idea-management and prioritisation approaches by the job buyers
   hire them for, not by feature checklist.
   - **Questions:** SQ1, SQ2, SQ7; hypotheses H7 and H9.
   - **Output:** `research/customer-market-evidence.md`.
   - **Verifies:** At least the status quo and material alternative categories
     are included; market claims have dated primary sources where available;
     absence of primary customer evidence remains visible.

3. **Compare public and internal modes at the user/trust level.** Identify
   actors, incentives, journeys, decisions, harms, data rights and observable
   value for each mode before testing shared primitives or a common brand.
   - **Questions:** SQ3, SQ9; hypotheses H3, H8 and H10.
   - **Output:** `research/product-modes-and-trust.md`.
   - **Verifies:** The public and internal journeys each include start, other-
     actor visibility, terminal outcome and no-action behaviour; anonymity and
     re-identification risks are not treated as architecture details.

4. **Evaluate relationship-aware differentiation and defensibility.** For each
   candidate capability, map the new signal, originating relationship, permitted
   use, consuming Sneat product, non-graph baseline, cold-start requirement and
   compounding mechanism. Reject “network effect” where value does not improve
   with participation or is readily portable/replicable.
   - **Questions:** SQ4–SQ6; hypotheses H1, H2, H4 and H6.
   - **Output:** `research/graph-value-and-defensibility.md`.
   - **Verifies:** Every claimed graph advantage names a measurable beneficiary
     and comparison; every cross-product use names its trust boundary; no data
     model or architecture is designed.

5. **Evaluate commercial model and go-to-market evidence.** Research buyer,
   purchasing trigger, value metric, pricing alternatives, acquisition paths,
   activation/retention loops and channel economics. Test LinkedIn as one
   candidate rather than a given.
   - **Questions:** SQ7–SQ8; hypotheses H5 and H7.
   - **Output:** `research/growth-and-commercial-evidence.md`.
   - **Verifies:** Channel recommendations cannot rest on audience fit alone;
     pricing intent is distinguished from willingness-to-pay evidence; product-
     led loops specify who receives immediate value.

6. **Synthesize strategic options only after research gates pass.** Compare the
   current scarce-priority direction, public-first, internal-first, separated
   products/modes, ecosystem-module paths, credible pivots and the status quo
   against agreed criteria.
   - **Dependencies:** Tasks 1–5.
   - **Outputs:** later `recommendations/business-strategy.md`,
     `recommendations/product-strategy.md` and
     `recommendations/go-to-market-strategy.md`.
   - **Verifies:** Each option carries evidence, assumptions, trade-offs,
     reversibility, risks, priority and an experiment or reviewed exemption;
     H1–H10 have explicit transitions.

7. **Challenge, resolve and edit independently.** The Devil's Advocate attempts
   to falsify the strongest position; the Managing Partner resolves or retains
   every material challenge; the Executive Editor then reconciles terminology,
   evidence, dissent and all sponsor asks.
   - **Dependencies:** Task 6.
   - **Outputs:** recommendation review sections, `decisions/`,
     `executive-summary.md`.
   - **Verifies:** Assurance roles did not author the recommendation; challenge
     precedes editing; every deliverable ledger row reaches evidence or an
     explicit sponsor-owned terminal state.

8. **Handoff, promote separately or repeat.** Present decision asks first. On
   acceptance, propose exact canonical deltas to their owners in a separate
   reviewed change; otherwise reopen the earliest affected stage and record an
   immutable checkpoint.
   - **Dependencies:** Task 7 and sponsor decision.
   - **Outputs:** terminal package, `checkpoints/`, optional promotion changes.
   - **Verifies:** Close leaves no work falsely active; share/replay works without
     this transcript; no engagement draft silently becomes canon.
