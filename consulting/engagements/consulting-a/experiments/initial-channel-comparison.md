# IssueNumber.one Controlled Initial-Channel Comparison Experiment

## Hypothesis

After a buyer, offer and paid product-transfer outcome are stable, either direct
trusted introduction or one fixed LinkedIn direct-contact mechanism may prove a
viable access route. A route-winner claim is allowed only when prior warmth and
account access are balanced well enough for a controlled comparison.

## Assumptions

- Buyer, problem, offer, qualification and paid-cycle price are fixed before the
  comparison.
- Eligible accounts can be assigned without using sensitive personal data or
  prohibited scraping/automation.
- Twenty eligible accounts per route can detect only an exploratory early
  signal; the thresholds are not calibrated estimates of conversion, scalable
  CAC or long-term incrementality.

## Smallest Possible Implementation

Construct a complete frame of 40 eligible accounts using the same segment rule
and offer. Before assignment, record founder/partner relationship strength,
whether each route is actually available, and any prior contact. Block or match
accounts on those factors and randomise between routes where both are feasible.
Assign 20 to each route only if the balance is credible:

- **A — direct trusted access:** founder relationship or authorised partner
  introduction with a personalised problem-led invitation;
- **B — LinkedIn direct contact:** one fixed, targeted, human-reviewed organic
  message with source attribution. Do not mix messages with posts, content
  distribution, landing-page discovery or paid media.

If equivalent allocation is not possible because access or warmth differs,
retain the same offer and measurement but report two separate route-feasibility
observations. Do not calculate or name a route winner.

Use the same follow-up limit, qualification script, proposal, price and paid
cycle. Track founder/partner time, tools, every eligible account, prior warmth,
contact attempts, refusals, exclusions, qualified problems, named buyers,
proposals, paid cycles, outcome activation and repeat. Keep withdrawn and
missing cases in the route denominator and report protocol deviations
separately.

Paid LinkedIn would add cost and creative variables before organic professional
access proves buyer/offer fit, so it is not the smallest first comparison.

## Owner, Cost and Duration

- **Owner:** Sponsor/founder as channel owner; Managing Partner or named growth
  analyst for measurement.
- **Participants:** 40 eligible professional accounts only after authorised
  outreach and prior product-transfer success.
- **Cost / budget source:** Founder/partner/contact time and ordinary tools;
  sponsor approves any incentive or partner fee. No paid-media budget.
- **Duration:** Four weeks of contact plus the paid-cycle/30-day repeat window.
- **Stop conditions:** Stop for complaints, platform-policy breach, contact-data
  uncertainty, route contamination, offer change, spam-like volume or material
  mismatch in account eligibility.

## Metrics and Guardrails

- **Exploratory viability for a route:** At least 4 qualified problems, 2
  proposals and 1 paid product-transfer cycle from its 20 eligible accounts,
  followed by the paid experiment's outcome/retention measurement.
- **Route winner:** Only when the matched/randomised allocation remains balanced,
  both routes satisfy viability, and fully loaded cost per qualified account is
  at least 30% lower with no lower paid-cycle or outcome activation count. A
  one-case removal or reclassification must not change the winner. One viable
  route and one non-viable route establishes provisional feasibility only—not a
  causal winner.
- **Failure:** Across all 40 accounts there is no paid cycle, fewer than four
  total qualified problems, or qualification reveals the offer/segment changed.
- **Inconclusive:** Both routes are viable within 30% cost; one route's sample is
  materially warmer/different despite assignment; equivalent allocation is not
  feasible; procurement extends beyond the window; one paid outcome cannot be
  compared yet; or one-case sensitivity changes the result.
- **Missing/refusal rule:** Report every eligible account, exclusion, refusal,
  withdrawal, missing outcome and protocol deviation by route. Do not replace a
  case after assignment or remove it from the denominator.
- **Guardrails / ethics:** No scraping, bulk automation, purchased personal
  lists, deceptive identity or dark patterns; honour opt-out; truthful claims;
  minimal professional contact data; no public customer reference without
  permission; separate attribution from causal claims.

## Expected Learning

The controlled comparison may choose the next bounded access route. An
unbalanced observation can only show whether each route is feasible. The result
applies only to direct trusted introductions and the tested human-reviewed
LinkedIn message; it does not validate LinkedIn content, paid LinkedIn, a
product-led loop or scalable CAC.

## Precommitted Decisions

- **On a robust winner:** Use that route for the next bounded cohort. Treat any
  paid LinkedIn or content test as a separate future experiment requiring a new
  mechanism and budget decision.
- **On route feasibility without a robust winner:** Report the viable route or
  routes, but make no strategic channel ranking.
- **On failure:** Stop channel optimisation and reopen buyer/offer evidence; do
  not compensate with broader reach.
- **If inconclusive:** Run no expansion or strategic route selection. A single
  predeclared replication is allowed only when allocation imbalance or one
  missing outcome is the stated ambiguity.
