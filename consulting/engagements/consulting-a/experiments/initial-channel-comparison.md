# IssueNumber.one Controlled Initial-Channel Comparison Experiment

## Hypothesis

After a buyer, offer and paid outcome are stable, one of two authorised access
routes—direct trusted founder/partner introduction or targeted LinkedIn organic
contact/content—produces qualified accounts and paid cycles at a materially
better fully loaded cost without lower outcome or repeat quality.

## Assumptions

- Buyer, problem, offer, qualification and paid-cycle price are fixed before the
  comparison.
- Eligible accounts can be assigned without using sensitive personal data or
  prohibited scraping/automation.
- Twenty eligible accounts per route can detect a strong early signal; the test
  cannot estimate scalable CAC or long-term incrementality.

## Smallest Possible Implementation

Preselect 40 eligible non-overlapping accounts using the same segment rule and
offer. Assign 20 to each route:

- **A — direct trusted access:** founder relationship or authorised partner
  introduction with a personalised problem-led invitation;
- **B — LinkedIn:** targeted, human-reviewed organic contact or a
  decision-useful post/landing path with source attribution. No paid media in
  this first comparison.

Use the same follow-up limit, qualification script, proposal, price and paid
cycle. Track founder/partner/content time, tools, eligible accounts, qualified
problems, named buyers, proposals, paid cycles, outcome activation and repeat.

Paid LinkedIn would add cost and creative variables before organic professional
access proves buyer/offer fit, so it is not the smallest first comparison.

## Owner, Cost and Duration

- **Owner:** Sponsor/founder as channel owner; Managing Partner or named growth
  analyst for measurement.
- **Participants:** 40 eligible professional accounts only after authorised
  outreach and prior paid-cycle success.
- **Cost / budget source:** Founder/partner/content time and ordinary tools;
  sponsor approves any incentive or partner fee. No paid-media budget.
- **Duration:** Four weeks of contact plus the paid-cycle/30-day repeat window.
- **Stop conditions:** Stop for complaints, platform-policy breach, contact-data
  uncertainty, route contamination, offer change, spam-like volume or material
  mismatch in account eligibility.

## Metrics and Guardrails

- **Success for a viable route:** At least 4 qualified problems, 2 proposals and
  1 paid cycle from its 20 eligible accounts, followed by the paid experiment's
  outcome/retention measurement.
- **Route winner:** Among viable routes, fully loaded cost per qualified account
  is at least 30% lower with no lower paid-cycle or outcome activation count. If
  only one route is viable, it wins provisionally.
- **Failure:** Across all 40 accounts there is no paid cycle, fewer than four
  total qualified problems, or qualification reveals the offer/segment changed.
- **Inconclusive:** Both routes are viable within 30% cost; one route's sample is
  materially warmer/different despite assignment; procurement extends beyond
  the window; or one paid outcome cannot be compared yet.
- **Guardrails / ethics:** No scraping, bulk automation, purchased personal
  lists, deceptive identity or dark patterns; honour opt-out; truthful claims;
  minimal professional contact data; no public customer reference without
  permission; separate attribution from causal claims.

## Expected Learning

The comparison can choose the next access route and reveal whether LinkedIn is
useful for qualified demand rather than raw attention. It does not validate paid
media, a product-led loop or scalable CAC.

## Precommitted Decisions

- **On success:** Use the provisional winning route for the next bounded cohort;
  consider paid LinkedIn only if LinkedIn is viable and buyer/offer economics can
  absorb a predeclared media test.
- **On failure:** Stop channel optimisation and reopen buyer/offer evidence; do
  not compensate with broader reach.
- **If inconclusive:** Keep both routes only if their fully loaded cost is
  acceptable against paid/retained value; otherwise run no expansion.
