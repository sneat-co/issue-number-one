# IssueNumber.one Relationship, Scarcity and Trust Lift Experiment

## Hypothesis

For one defined internal priority decision, a scarce issue/support mechanism
with visible disposition improves decision quality or time over the current
manual/non-graph workflow, and one verified responsibility relationship further
improves correct routing without reducing trust comprehension or increasing
identity inference.

## Assumptions

- A synthetic operational scenario can have a defensible decision/routing
  rubric without using real employee issues.
- Relevant decision users can distinguish a meaningful workflow difference in a
  small crossover pilot.
- Relationship context is tested one field at a time; an entire graph cannot be
  treated as one experimental variable.

## Smallest Possible Implementation

Create one synthetic cross-functional decision with the same issue evidence in
three variants:

- **A — current/manual baseline:** ordinary submissions plus meeting or manual
  prioritisation;
- **B — scarcity/action:** bounded issues and support plus explicit possible
  dispositions and terminal response;
- **C — bounded relationship context:** B plus one verified accountable-unit or
  workflow-owner relationship necessary for routing.

Run a randomised-order, within-participant pilot with 12 relevant decision
owners. Ask each to choose the priority/action, route it, explain limitations,
and predict who can see identity/content, what crosses products, what happens on
silence and what the terminal state means. Record decision-rubric score, routing
accuracy, time, unsafe identity guesses and trust-comprehension answers.

This is smaller than a product build and separates scarcity/action from
relationship context. A single bundled mock would not identify the mechanism.

## Owner, Cost and Duration

- **Owner:** Managing Partner or named product researcher; sponsor authorises
  participants.
- **Participants:** 12 decision owners matching the successful discovery job;
  no employees reporting sensitive concerns.
- **Cost / budget source:** Approximately 15–20 research hours plus optional
  sponsor-approved incentives; no budget approved.
- **Duration:** Two weeks after the discovery success gate.
- **Stop conditions:** Stop for real confidential data, participant distress,
  inability to define a valid decision rubric, or evidence that the scenario
  embeds the desired answer.

## Metrics and Guardrails

- **Success for B over A:** Median completion time improves by at least 20%
  without reducing decision-rubric score; at least 10 of 12 correctly predict
  recipient, null-action and terminal-state rules.
- **Success for C over B:** The explicit relationship changes an otherwise
  ambiguous route to the rubric-correct owner for at least 4 of 12 participants,
  does not lower decision score or trust comprehension, and creates zero
  additional unsafe identity guesses.
- **Failure:** B improves median time by less than 10% with no decision-quality
  gain; or C produces no routing improvement; or either variant increases false
  identity, reuse, representativeness or terminal-state beliefs.
- **Inconclusive:** Time improvement is 10–19%; decision scores conflict with
  speed; fewer than 10 participants understand the shared promise for reasons
  traceable to wording; or the routing ambiguity is too weak to discriminate.
- **Guardrails / ethics:** Synthetic non-sensitive content only; randomise order;
  predeclare rubric before sessions; no concept-preference success metric; no
  claim of statistical population inference; capture de-identified results;
  sponsor approval before contact.

## Expected Learning

The experiment distinguishes four possibilities: scarcity helps; visible action
helps; relationship context adds incremental routing value; or the ordinary
workflow is sufficient. It also tests whether the trust promise survives the
concept before any implementation.

## Precommitted Decisions

- **On success:** Preserve only the mechanisms that pass and allow the paid
  concierge cycle to test them in a real bounded decision.
- **On failure:** Remove failed scarcity or relationship claims from the paid
  offer; if the action loop alone adds no value, stop O2.
- **If inconclusive:** Correct only the identified wording/rubric problem and
  repeat once; do not build to manufacture stronger evidence.
