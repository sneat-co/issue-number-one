# IssueNumber.one Paid Concierge Priority-to-Action Experiment

## Hypothesis

After buyer/problem and mechanism gates pass, a leader will first pay for a
manually facilitated bounded priority-to-action cycle and use it in a real
decision. A separate product-transfer hypothesis is that the organisation will
then pay for and complete a second cycle without founder facilitation at a price
that covers the full marginal delivery and support cost.

## Assumptions

- A manual facilitated cycle can deliver the promised outcome without a product
  build.
- The operational decision can avoid employee-sensitive, safeguarding,
  performance or health content in this first test.
- The sponsor predeclares one fixed price no lower than the fully loaded marginal
  delivery and support cost, excluding research-learning overhead.
- Three organisations can test payment/action/repeat behaviour, not estimate a
  conversion rate or market size.
- All thresholds are exploratory decision rules, not calibrated estimates of
  market demand, conversion or retention.

## Smallest Possible Implementation

Run two explicitly separated phases with three qualifying organisations from
the successful discovery cohort.

### Phase 1 — facilitated service

Offer the same fixed-scope paid concierge cycle:

1. define one decision, owner, participants, close date and trust promise;
2. collect a bounded number of issues and scarce support through the smallest
   safe manual tool/process;
3. facilitate the decision owner response;
4. publish safe acknowledgement/action/defer/reject dispositions;
5. close and review the actual decision and contributor-visible outcome;
6. offer the same next cycle at the same commercial principle.

For every offer, record exactly one mutually exclusive commercial state:

- **funds received**;
- **signed unconditional paid contract** with price, scope and start date;
- **non-binding budget or procurement intent**; or
- **no paid commitment**.

Only funds received and a signed unconditional paid contract count as payment.
A procurement statement, unsigned order, conditional pilot, free trial,
discounted learning favour or verbal intent does not count.

### Phase 2 — product transfer

Only after a Phase 1 organisation completes its first cycle, offer an equivalent
second paid cycle that must complete without founder facilitation. Record all
delivery-tool, onboarding, support, exception-handling and sponsor time. The
price must cover the full marginal cost of those activities. A founder-led
second workshop remains service evidence and cannot satisfy this phase.

Do not import a broad relationship graph or collect anonymous employee
concerns. Phase 1 uses existing tools and manual facilitation because service
payment and outcome are the questions. Phase 2 may use the smallest transferable
workflow required to remove founder facilitation, but it does not authorise a
platform build or choose a standalone-versus-Sneat Work boundary.

## Owner, Cost and Duration

- **Owner:** Sponsor/founder as commercial owner; named facilitator/researcher
  for protocol and evidence.
- **Participants:** Three authorised organisations, each with a decision owner
  and bounded contributor cohort. Record whether each organisation is non-warm,
  weakly connected or strongly connected to the founder before contact.
- **Cost / budget source:** Sponsor preapproves delivery time and any tools; each
  organisation pays the predeclared non-token price. No budget is approved.
- **Duration:** Up to eight weeks across sale, three cycles and repeat offers.
- **Stop conditions:** Stop any cycle for sensitive employee/safeguarding data,
  unclear decision authority, false anonymity expectations, coercive
  participation, unpaid scope expansion or inability to provide terminal state.

## Metrics and Guardrails

- **Phase 1 service success:** At least 2 of 3 organisations reach a counted
  payment state before delivery, complete the real decision, and publish safe
  dispositions for all material top issues. This validates only a facilitated
  service outcome—not software willingness to pay or a product boundary.
- **Phase 2 product-transfer success:** At least 2 organisations pay for a
  second cycle, complete it without founder facilitation, and do so at a price
  covering the full marginal delivery and support cost.
- **Generalisability guard:** At least 2 successful payment cases must be
  non-warm. Warm-only success is reported only as founder-network feasibility.
- **Failure:** No organisation reaches a counted payment state; or paid cycles
  do not change/confirm a documented decision; or safe visible dispositions
  cannot be provided. Phase 2 fails if, after the permitted follow-up, fewer
  than 2 organisations pay for and complete the founder-independent second
  cycle at cost-covering price.
- **Inconclusive:** Exactly 1 organisation satisfies a phase; a procurement
  process extends beyond the window despite named budget; or one cycle is
  invalidated by a predeclared execution deviation rather than the value
  proposition. A procurement case may remain inconclusive once; after the one
  permitted bounded follow-up, absence of funds or an unconditional contract is
  failure.
- **Sensitivity and missing data:** Show how every conclusion changes if one
  counted case is removed or reclassified. If that changes the decision, report
  the result as inconclusive. Withdrawn, missing or protocol-invalid cases stay
  in the denominator and are reported separately; they are not replaced
  silently.
- **Guardrails / ethics:** Operational/non-sensitive scope only; explicit
  participation and attribution promise; no hidden worker monitoring; separate
  safeguarding route; de-identified engagement evidence; customer controls its
  operational records; no fabricated case study/publication; sponsor-approved
  contracts and retention.

## Expected Learning

Phase 1 tests payment for a facilitated service, real decision value and
contributor-visible action. Phase 2 separately tests product transfer, repeat
payment and marginal delivery/support cost. Neither phase alone proves scalable
demand, a graph advantage or the correct product boundary.

## Precommitted Decisions

- **On Phase 1 success only:** Retain the facilitated service as evidence and do
  not infer product willingness to pay or choose a product boundary.
- **On Phase 2 success:** Bring the service-versus-standalone-versus-Sneat Work
  boundary to a separate evidence review using observed entry, context, support
  and repeat behaviour; do not add public/benchmark modes.
- **On failure:** Stop the failed phase. If Phase 1 fails, park the
  priority-to-action direction for at least 90 days unless materially new
  evidence appears. If Phase 2 fails, do not fund a product implementation from
  service evidence.
- **If inconclusive:** Run at most one additional comparable case only when the
  ambiguity is procurement or execution-specific and the protocol change is
  predeclared. Otherwise stop and retain the unresolved boundary.
