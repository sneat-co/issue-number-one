# IssuerNumber.One Customer and Market Evidence

## Owned Question

What urgent, repeated, budget-backed customer problem exists, for which initial
user and buyer; what do they do today; and which alternatives would they
compare with IssueNumber.one?

This report addresses SQ1, SQ2 and SQ7, and tests H7 and H9. It is advisory
research only: it neither selects a segment nor recommends a product strategy.

## Concise Answer

Public evidence supports a **candidate paid problem**, not a validated
IssuerNumber.one wedge: People/HR leaders and line managers repeatedly need to
turn employee feedback into credible, confidential and actionable priorities.
The problem is plausibly urgent where retention, wellbeing or change outcomes
are at risk: HSE estimates 22.1 million Great Britain working days were lost to
work-related stress, depression or anxiety in 2024/25, while Gallup reports
that 40% of employees globally experienced a lot of stress the preceding day
in 2025. These sources do **not** show that an issue/vote tool causes better
outcomes. [HSE working-days statistics](https://www.hse.gov.uk/statistics/dayslost.htm),
[Gallup 2026 global data](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-global-data.aspx)

There is clear category spend: Lattice publicly prices employee engagement at
$4 per seat/month with a $4,000 annual minimum; Workday Peakon sells employee
listening, benchmarks and action planning; and product teams can buy discovery,
idea and prioritisation tools such as Jira Product Discovery, Aha! and Canny.
That establishes that organisations pay for adjacent jobs, **not** that they
would pay IssuerNumber.one, pay for peer issue benchmarks, or prefer scarce
votes to their current workflow. [Lattice pricing](https://lattice.com/pricing),
[Workday Peakon overview](https://www.workday.com/en-us/products/employee-voice/overview.html),
[Jira Product Discovery pricing](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/product-discovery/pricing),
[Aha! pricing](https://www.aha.io/pricing), [Canny pricing](https://canny.io/pricing)

The most defensible public conclusion is therefore conditional: the initial
candidate user is a People/HR or team leader who must convert recurring staff
signals into an owned action, and the likely economic buyer is the People/HR
leader (or an existing HR-suite owner). This is an inference from the adjacent
products' workflows and prices, not customer evidence. Public-sector
consultation is a separate candidate market with decision-makers constrained by
consultation practice and inclusion, but its buyer, evidence standards and
respondent incentives are materially different. It must not be treated as the
same market merely because both collect issues.

## Findings

### Observed or sourced

- **A recurring organisational problem exists, but it is broad.** E1 and E2
  document significant workplace wellbeing/engagement signals. E3 and E4 show
  that vendors package the operational job as repeated listening, analysis,
  benchmarking and action planning. This supports a candidate problem of
  converting feedback into accountable action—not a claim that every stressed
  workforce needs another listening tool.
- **Budget-backed alternatives already span the full workflow.** Employee
  listening (E3–E4) covers staff pulse/survey through action; product discovery
  and idea management (E5–E7) cover capture, deduplication, prioritisation and
  delivery linkage; and public/panel research (E8–E9) covers recruitment,
  sampling, participation and accountable consultation. The buyer's comparison
  set will depend on the decision being made, rather than the generic label
  "issue intelligence."
- **Status quo is a real competing workflow.** GOV.UK guidance anticipates
  online consultation alongside email, letters, phone, meetings and other mixed
  methods; product tools market integrations with existing Jira, service-desk,
  chat and sales/support systems (E5, E7, E9). This supports treating manual
  collection, spreadsheet/theme synthesis, leadership/manager meetings and an
  existing HRIS or work-management suite as alternatives—not as an absence of a
  solution.
- **Benchmarking is an established feature, not proof of a willingness-to-pay
  gap.** Workday Peakon's benchmark material and Lattice's separate compensation
  benchmark offering show that peer context is sold in HR technology (E3–E4).
  They do not establish that customers will trust, share data for, or pay for a
  cross-organisation *issue* benchmark. This leaves H7 unresolved.
- **Public consultation is a different job-to-be-done.** E8–E9 indicate that
  public research/consultation buyers must define a decision, audience,
  inclusion and method, and often need paid recruitment or governed reporting.
  Raw voting or a public issue board cannot by itself evidence representative
  public opinion.

### Inferred

- **Candidate internal job and actors (E1–E4):** A People/HR leader buys—or
  expands an HCM/listening tool—when the organisation needs a repeatable way to
  hear employees, protect enough confidentiality for honest input, identify
  priorities and hold managers to action. Employees are the data-contributing
  user; managers are action users; People/HR is the likely economic owner.
  This remains an inference because no target organisation has confirmed its
  workflow, trigger or buying authority.
- **Candidate product-team job (E5–E7):** A product leader may pay to collapse
  scattered customer feedback into prioritised, delivery-linked decisions. The
  current scarce-issue/scarce-vote mechanism could be compared here as one
  prioritisation instrument, but E5–E7 do not show it is the decisive unmet
  instrument. H9 remains unresolved.
- **Candidate public job (E8–E9):** A public body may have a repeated need to
  gather and explain consideration of public input. It is not safe to infer that
  an open community or respondent panel produces comparable evidence, or that a
  single brand should serve this buyer and the internal buyer.
- **No initial buyer can be selected from public evidence.** The presence of
  mature alternatives and list-price spend is evidence of category competition,
  not evidence of an underserved segment or a switch trigger. The smallest
  supportable claim is a shortlist of candidate jobs above.

## Contradictions and Disconfirming Evidence

- The same evidence that shows budget also shows entrenched, bundled
  alternatives: Lattice combines engagement with reviews, goals and manager
  tools; Workday combines employee voice with HCM context; Jira Product
  Discovery links ideas to Jira and service management (E3–E5). A standalone
  issue product therefore has an unproven displacement problem.
- HSE and Gallup establish prevalence/cost of workplace signals, not that
  feedback software reduces stress, turnover or lost days (E1–E2). Vendor
  outcome language is not treated as causal evidence.
- Benchmarks are offered by employee-listening vendors (E4), while public
  guidance demands deliberately selected audiences, accessibility and
  proportionate methods (E9). This conflicts with any premise that pooled votes
  automatically create a credible cross-organisation benchmark.
- Public research/panel buyers pay separately for recruitment and sampling (E8),
  whereas an internal employee-listening programme starts with a known
  workforce. These are different data-generating processes and should not be
  collapsed into one demand claim.
- Exact public searches for the product name did not yield independently
  attributable traction, customer stories, purchasing evidence or usage data;
  that absence is scope-limited and does not prove absence of demand (E10).

## Assumptions and Unknowns

- **Assumption:** The mandate's `IssuerNumber.One` refers to the repository's
  `IssueNumber.one`. If false, all canonical references and public-name searches
  need rerouting.
- **Unknown (highest leverage):** Existing aggregate demand evidence—waitlist
  counts by month, conversion, selected plans, qualified conversations, repeat
  use and budget-owner intent. It changes whether any candidate job has an
  internal signal. Safe default: make no traction claim (E10).
- **Unknown:** For the candidate internal job, whether the first buyer is a
  People/HR leader, a business-unit manager, an existing HCM owner or a product
  team; and what trigger makes the current workflow fail. This changes the
  category comparison and value metric.
- **Unknown:** Whether scarce issue/vote limits produce faster, fairer or more
  actioned priorities than an existing survey, manager process or discovery
  tool. This is the key falsifier for H9.
- **Unknown:** Whether organisations will permit, trust and pay for peer issue
  benchmarks after accounting for comparability, consent, confidentiality and
  data rights. H7 is unvalidated; a vendor offering a benchmark is not enough.
- **Unknown:** Whether public consultation is an economically or ethically
  compatible initial market. Its sampling, inclusion and statutory constraints
  may make it a separate product decision.

## Confidence

**Low-medium.** Confidence is medium that paid alternatives and the broad
employee-voice/public-engagement jobs exist, because current public primary
sources show list prices, products and government practice. Confidence is low
that the problem is urgent, repeated and budget-backed for a specific
IssuerNumber.one initial user/buyer, or that the current scarce-vote mechanism
is the answer. The result would change most with authorized non-identifying
demand aggregates and several structured problem interviews that compare the
actual current workflow, switching trigger, buyer and budget source.

## Risks, Dependencies and Recommended Research Priority

1. **Priority 1 — establish demand before segment choice.** The report cannot
   distinguish a founder-concept market from an existing demand signal. It
   depends on sponsor authorization to provide the non-identifying evidence
   inventory named in the engagement brief; it does not require raw waitlist
   data.
2. **Priority 2 — discover the workflow, not feature preference.** For each
   candidate job, determine the latest real decision, how input arrived, who
   consolidated it, what action followed, what broke, who had budget authority,
   and what existing tools/contracts would be compared. This requires separate
   outreach/participant authorization.
3. **Priority 3 — keep internal listening and public research separate until
   evidence says otherwise.** A future product/trust review should test privacy,
   representativeness and action-accountability requirements. The dependency is
   a defined audience and trust boundary, not architecture work.
4. **Risk:** Treating employee stress/engagement prevalence as proof that a new
   feedback tool will create value could lead to a false positive. **Mitigation:**
   require a comparative current-workflow and action-outcome baseline.
5. **Risk:** Treating vendor list prices as willingness to pay for a benchmark
   or scarce-vote feature could overstate H7/H9. **Mitigation:** ask for paid
   alternatives, contract renewal/switching evidence and a concrete budget
   owner before a material claim.

## Proposed Smallest Learning Actions

- **Not an experiment:** ask the sponsor to supply or authorize the engagement
  brief's non-identifying demand inventory: monthly unique waitlist totals,
  aggregate plan/source counts, aggregate traffic/conversion, de-identified
  past-problem-conversation summaries, and any evidence of repeat use, purchase
  intent or budget ownership. This is the smallest safe action because it can
  materially update the starting segment without contact or personal data.
- **If later authorized, a bounded discovery action:** interview a small,
  deliberately varied set of candidate People/HR leaders and product leaders
  about a recent feedback-to-action decision. Success is not positive sentiment;
  it is evidence that the same urgent workflow, buyer and budget recurs and that
  an existing alternative fails in a comparable way. Stop if workflows split by
  mode or the buyer cannot name an action/budget consequence.
- **No product experiment is proposed now.** The necessary target job, buyer,
  baseline and falsifiable value metric are not yet evidenced; labelling a
  landing or vote mechanic as a validation experiment would be misleading.

## Dissent and Conflicts for Managing Partner

- The strongest positive reading is that a paid employee-listening category
  plus workplace-cost evidence justifies an internal organisational-intelligence
  candidate. The counter-reading is stronger on the present evidence: mature,
  integrated suites may mean there is no sufficiently underserved problem for a
  standalone product. Both readings remain live.
- Public consultation/research has real governance and spending mechanisms, but
  it should be treated as a separate candidate problem, not supporting evidence
  for an internal employee-voice wedge or shared benchmark network.
- H7 and H9 are **unresolved**, not supported: peer-benchmark products and
  prioritisation tools prove adjacent willingness to pay, not willingness to
  pay for IssueNumber.one's proposed benchmark or scarcity mechanism.
