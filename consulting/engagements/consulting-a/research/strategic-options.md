# IssueNumber.one Strategic Options Synthesis

## Owned Question

Which strategic direction best fits the available evidence, including the
status quo and credible pivots, without treating founder hypotheses, adjacent
category spend or ecosystem reuse as validated demand?

## Concise Answer

No option has enough evidence for an unconditional commitment. The strongest
**provisional learning question** is whether a narrow internal priority-to-
action job exists for bounded cross-functional teams. Current evidence does not
order a facilitator-led service, a standalone scarce-priority tool or a Sneat
Work capability within that question; no-build remains the decision control.

This question ranks first on **reversibility, cold-start and trust burden**, not
on demonstrated demand. It can test IssueNumber.one's scarce issue/vote idea,
an accountable action loop and bounded explicit work context independently,
without a public network or benchmark cohort. It deliberately does not claim
employee listening, organisational health, public opinion, benchmarking, a
graph moat or a two-sided network effect.

The direction remains low-confidence. No target buyer has confirmed the job,
no team has repeated the cycle, no action outcome or payment has been observed,
and the current landing tests a different proposition. The correct commitment
is therefore to an evidence sequence and kill criteria, not a platform build.

## Decision Criteria

The criteria are ordered by consequence; qualitative ratings avoid false
precision where evidence is absent.

1. **Buyer/problem evidence:** Is there a repeated consequential decision, user,
   economic buyer, current alternative and budget trigger?
2. **Observable outcome:** Can one bounded use produce a result another actor
   sees and acts on, including null action and terminal closure?
3. **Trust and method fitness:** Can the job be served without false anonymity,
   surveillance, false representativeness or context collapse?
4. **Cold-start feasibility:** Can value exist before a public network,
   cross-organisation dataset or large cohort?
5. **Differentiation:** Is there a falsifiable advantage over forms, surveys,
   work trackers, listening suites and manual leadership practice?
6. **Ecosystem fit without silo creation:** Can the capability use or contribute
   a necessary, purpose-bound signal without forcing unsafe graph reuse?
7. **Commercial and distribution plausibility:** Can a named buyer be reached,
   qualified, charged and retained through a measurable path?
8. **Reversibility and learning speed:** Can the thesis be tested manually or
   synthetically before product, brand or data commitments?

## Evidence Constraints Shared by Every Option

- **Observed:** The live landing and waitlist instrument exist, but results were
  not supplied and the application is still a scaffold. See the
  [current-state baseline](current-state-and-demand-evidence.md).
- **Sourced:** Organisations pay for adjacent employee-listening, discovery,
  survey and research workflows; no source proves an IssueNumber.one wedge.
  See [customer and market evidence](customer-market-evidence.md).
- **Observed/sourced:** Public research and internal organisation intelligence
  have materially different users, legitimacy, trust and action requirements.
  See [product modes and trust](product-modes-and-trust.md).
- **Inferred from sourced mechanisms:** Bounded relationship context can be
  useful, while no graph lift, network effect, switching cost or benchmark moat
  is observed. See [graph value and defensibility](graph-value-and-defensibility.md).
- **Observed/sourced:** LinkedIn is instrumentable but no channel economics,
  buyer conversion or retained revenue is available. See
  [growth and commercial evidence](growth-and-commercial-evidence.md).

## Option Set

### O0 — No product commitment; continue research only

Preserve the code/spec/landing assets but make no product, positioning, channel
or platform commitment until buyer and demand evidence exists.

- **Strengths:** Highest reversibility; prevents building on concept-only
  evidence; keeps trust risk at zero.
- **Weaknesses:** Produces no behavioural or payment evidence unless research is
  authorised; opportunity cost may continue indefinitely.
- **Best case:** Sponsor-supplied aggregates or discovery quickly identify a
  real job and buyer.
- **Failure mode:** “More research” becomes a non-decision with no deadline or
  falsifier.

### O1 — Standalone scarce-priority tool

Retain IssueNumber.one as a standalone tool whose core job is forcing a team to
name few issues and spend few votes, without intelligence, graph, benchmark or
public-opinion claims.

- **Strengths:** Closest to current canon and landing; low conceptual scope;
  clean non-graph baseline; possible immediate value in one cycle.
- **Weaknesses:** Buyer/action outcome and repeated use are unevidenced;
  standalone distribution and integration compete with forms, work trackers and
  product-discovery tools.
- **Best case:** Scarcity produces materially better decisions and repeat use.
- **Failure mode:** It is a novel voting mechanic that does not change action or
  justify another tool.

### O2 — Internal priority-to-action capability inside Sneat Work

Use scarce issues/votes for bounded team or cross-functional decisions, add a
visible accountable response/closure loop, and use only necessary explicit work
context for scope or routing. Treat IssueNumber.one as a capability/proposition
within Sneat Work rather than assuming an independent platform boundary.

- **Strengths:** Preserves the current differentiator; value can start with one
  team and one decision; action history may be more useful and less sensitive
  than a person-centred issue graph; existing Sneat Work context may reduce
  setup friction.
- **Weaknesses:** No buyer, entry path or outcome is validated; bundling may hide
  the proposition or import dependence on Sneat Work adoption; integration is
  not itself customer value.
- **Best case:** A department/product/engineering/operations leader pays because
  contested priorities become an owned action and the cycle repeats.
- **Failure mode:** Users participate but leaders do not act differently, pay or
  repeat; manual forms/work trackers remain sufficient.

### O3 — Internal employee-listening and organisation-intelligence platform

Target People/HR and managers with anonymous/confidential feedback, themes,
organisational blockers, health insights and action planning.

- **Strengths:** Adjacent category has visible budget and recurring workflows;
  internal cohorts and action planning are established practices.
- **Weaknesses:** Mature suites bundle listening, benchmarks and HCM context;
  psychological safety, employment power, re-identification and action debt
  create a high trust bar; no wedge or buyer switch trigger is known.
- **Best case:** A neglected segment values constrained issue/action workflow
  enough to displace or complement a suite.
- **Failure mode:** Another survey/listening tool increases collection burden or
  harm without changing action.

### O4 — Public participation or research product

Serve public/community issue collection, consultation, opinion grouping or
research reports, with the exact method named rather than conflated.

- **Strengths:** Public distribution and shareable outcomes are possible;
  governed consultation/research has real buyers and budgets; a public process
  can create visible evidence.
- **Weaknesses:** Open participation, consultation and population research are
  different products/jobs; sampling, inclusion, moderation, accountability and
  methodology are substantial; public sharing has no evidenced product loop.
- **Best case:** A commissioner funds a defensible process/report and repeats.
- **Failure mode:** A popular board is misread as public opinion, captured by a
  mobilised group, or attracts attention without a paying sponsor.

### O5 — Cross-organisation benchmark and data network

Collect comparable issue/organisation data and sell peer benchmarks whose value
is expected to improve with participation.

- **Strengths:** Peer context is sold in adjacent categories; a valid cohort can
  improve coverage and decision context.
- **Weaknesses:** No stable instrument, eligible population, peer definition,
  contribution permission, minimum protected cohort, buyer decision or payment
  exists; opt-in data can compound bias and disclosure rather than value.
- **Best case:** More eligible comparable organisations improve decision use and
  renewal while protecting contributors.
- **Failure mode:** The dataset is methodologically incompatible, portable or
  unsafe and does not change a buyer decision.

### O6 — One broad relationship-aware issue-intelligence platform

Combine public research, internal intelligence, benchmarking, Moodometer and
cross-product graph signals under one platform/brand.

- **Strengths:** A coherent high-level narrative and theoretical reuse of shared
  accounts, relationships and issue primitives.
- **Weaknesses:** Highest evidence, trust, cold-start and brand burden; shared
  primitives do not resolve different buyers/methods; H6 is rejected in its
  universal form; every monetisation and network-effect claim is unvalidated.
- **Best case:** Multiple modes reinforce a safe, unique data asset and shared
  distribution.
- **Failure mode:** Context collapse, siloed modes behind one label, broad scope
  before demand, or graph accumulation without user value.

### O7 — Separate public and internal products from the outset

Develop distinct brands/products and trust domains with only carefully governed
common primitives.

- **Strengths:** Clearer legitimacy, buyer, method and data-use promises.
- **Weaknesses:** Two unvalidated markets, two cold starts and duplicated
  discovery before either job is proven; premature product/brand structure.
- **Best case:** Both modes prove distinct budget-backed jobs and benefit from
  separation.
- **Failure mode:** Organisational complexity grows around two concept-only
  products.

## Resolution

The **boundary-neutral priority-to-action job** is the strongest provisional
learning direction, but O0 is the decision control. O1, O2 and a facilitator-
led service are observationally equivalent on current evidence; no product or
Sneat Work boundary is selected.

The deciding logic is:

1. The candidate job can create a terminal other-actor outcome—an accountable
   response—rather than stopping at collection or ranking.
2. It can start inside one bounded decision cycle, avoiding O4/O5/O6 cold-start
   and method claims.
3. It can test scarcity, accountable action and explicit context separately
   before deciding whether a service, standalone tool or Sneat Work capability
   carries the value.
4. It can keep relationship context explicit and purpose-bound, avoiding O3's
   broad monitoring/health interpretation and H6's rejected universal reuse.
5. It is reversible if tested manually before any platform, brand, architecture
   or cross-product data commitment.

This is not evidence that the internal job, O1 or O2 will win. It is a risk-and-
learning ordering only; O3 and O4 remain untested on relative demand.

## Sequence and Decision Gates

1. **Evidence inventory gate:** Before participant contact, accept privacy-
   preserving existing evidence or record the sponsor's explicit decline/
   unavailability. Otherwise preserve “traction unknown.”
2. **Problem/buyer gate:** A recent recurring priority-to-action decision, named
   buyer, current alternative and consequence must recur across target accounts.
   If not, stop the candidate job and park it for 90 days unless materially new
   evidence arrives.
3. **Mechanism gate:** Scarcity and visible action must be separated before one
   explicit-context variant is compared with the winning non-relationship
   baseline.
4. **Service/payment gate:** Paid facilitated delivery establishes only a
   service hypothesis. Product willingness requires at least two independently
   paid repeat cycles completed without founder facilitation at full marginal
   delivery/support cost.
5. **Boundary gate:** Before O2 outranks O1/service, at least two paid repeated
   cycles must show a necessary explicit Sneat Work relationship improving the
   buyer-valued outcome over manual/standalone context.
6. **Expansion gate:** Public, employee-intelligence and benchmark options remain
   not tested on demand; success here cannot reject or reopen them automatically.

## Risks and Dissent

- Choosing O2 may reflect ecosystem convenience rather than customer demand.
  The problem/buyer and payment gates exist specifically to falsify that bias.
- Product/engineering/operations leaders are plausible actors, not validated
  buyers; People/HR adjacent spend does not transfer automatically.
- A visible action loop may be the real value while scarce voting and graph
  context add little. The mechanism comparison must allow that result.
- Sneat Work context can reduce setup but also make switching, positioning and
  trust harder. The standalone O1 comparator must remain credible.
- O4 may ultimately be the better business if a funded commissioner and method
  emerge. It is ranked lower now because public research is not one coherent job
  and no sponsor/customer evidence exists.
- O5 could become valuable later, but starting with a benchmark would require
  the very cohort, method, trust and buyer evidence the engagement lacks.

## Confidence and Next Evidence

**Low confidence.** The ranking is based on relative reversibility, cold start,
trust burden and alignment with current assets—not observed demand or outcomes.

Evidence most likely to change the ranking:

- sponsor-provided aggregate demand/use/payment evidence for any mode;
- repeated recent priority-to-action failures with a named buyer and budget;
- a funded public research/consultation sponsor with a defined method;
- a graph/non-graph comparison showing material decision lift or harm;
- a paid repeat cycle that clarifies standalone versus Sneat Work entry.

## Proposed Experiments

The material direction requires three later contracts before commitment:

1. problem/buyer discovery across O0/O1/O2 and credible alternative jobs;
2. a synthetic/manual relationship-and-scarcity mechanism comparison;
3. a paid concierge priority-to-action cycle followed, only if justified, by a
   controlled channel comparison.

No experiment is authorised or executed by this synthesis. Complete contracts
belong in `../experiments/` and require sponsor approval for participants,
outreach, budget and evidence retention.
