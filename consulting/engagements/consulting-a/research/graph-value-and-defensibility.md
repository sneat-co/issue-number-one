# IssueNumber.one Graph Value and Defensibility Research

## Owned Question

Do relationship context or an issue-relationship graph produce measurable user
value or a defensible business advantage over the current scarce-issue/vote
baseline and credible non-graph alternatives?

This report completes accepted work-plan task 4 and tests H1, H2, H4 and H6. It
does not choose a product, business or go-to-market strategy and does not design
an architecture.

## Concise Answer

The present evidence does **not** establish that an issue relationship graph is
IssueNumber.one's durable asset or that it creates a network effect.

Relationship context has plausible, bounded user value when an explicit and
permitted relationship answers a decision question: who can act, which affected
cohort is missing, where an issue crosses organisational boundaries, or whether
materially different groups disagree. No comparison shows that this context
produces a better, safer or faster decision than the same issues, scarce votes
and action history with manual or explicit non-graph fields.

The mechanisms commonly grouped under “graph advantage” remain distinct:

- no direct user-to-user network effect is evidenced;
- a contributor/action-owner cross-side effect is a testable hypothesis, not an
  observed flywheel;
- public sharing is currently a distribution-loop hypothesis, not a network
  effect;
- cross-organisation benchmarking could gain coverage from additional
  *comparable* organisations, while additional biased, incompatible or finely
  sliced data can worsen validity and disclosure risk;
- issue/action history could support learning or institutional memory, but no
  learning curve, exclusivity, retention lift or switching cost is measured;
- reuse of an existing Sneat platform graph is shared infrastructure and may
  reduce implementation or routing friction; it is not by itself customer value
  or defensibility.

H6 is contradicted as a universal claim. Several capabilities produce signals
with no evidenced safe cross-product beneficiary and strong reasons for
isolation. H1, H2 and H4 remain unresolved; evidence currently weighs against
promoting H2's graph-as-primary-asset framing or H4's network-effect language.

## Method and Scope

Research performed on 2026-08-04:

1. Reconciled the four reviewed engagement reports named in `dependencies` and
   the accepted H1/H2/H4/H6 definitions.
2. Compared the candidate mechanisms with economic research on two-sided
   platforms, critical mass, data-enabled learning and non-rival data.
3. Checked current official sources on survey validity, portability,
   interoperability and switching.
4. Required every candidate advantage to name a beneficiary, signal and trust
   domain, non-graph baseline, compounding mechanism, measurable prediction,
   evidence, counterevidence and falsifier.
5. Searched exact product/claim combinations for IssueNumber.one graph lift,
   benchmark methodology, minimum viable cohorts, switching cost and
   cross-product outcome evidence. No attributable result was found in the
   bounded searches; “not found” is not proof that no private evidence exists.

No private data, user research, experiment, contact, campaign or canonical
source was accessed or changed. Economic papers define mechanisms and
conditions; they do not establish that IssueNumber.one satisfies them. The EU
and OECD portability sources are comparison evidence about contestability, not
deployment-specific legal conclusions for this product.

## Mechanism Taxonomy

| Mechanism | What must improve as participation/data grows | Present IssueNumber.one evidence | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incremental user value | A named decision becomes better, safer, faster or less costly than the non-graph baseline | Plausible routing/cohort cases; no comparative outcome (E1–E3) | Unresolved |
| Direct network effect | Another user of the same type directly increases a user's utility | No interaction or utility curve identified | Not evidenced |
| Cross-side network effect | More contributors increase value to action owners and more capable action owners increase value to contributors | Roles are plausible; reciprocal participation/value and retention are unmeasured (E4) | Hypothesis only |
| Data-enabled learning | Additional within- or across-organisation examples measurably improve output quality | No model, learning task, benchmark or quality curve (E6) | Not evidenced |
| Economies of scale | Average delivery/research cost falls while quality remains acceptable | No cost or operations baseline | Not evidenced |
| Switching cost | Retained history, workflow or relationships make replacement materially costly for a satisfied customer | No switching event, export burden or retention evidence; portability is a counterforce (E7, E9–E10) | Unresolved; cannot be inferred |
| Distribution loop | Existing use causes a recipient to encounter and adopt the product | Public sharing and invitations are founder hypotheses; recipient-owned value is unmeasured | Hypothesis only, not a network effect |
| Shared infrastructure | Existing Sneat platform identity/relationship capabilities reduce duplicated work | A graph substrate exists; no incremental customer outcome or exclusionary advantage is shown (E3) | Possible reuse benefit, not defensibility evidence |

## Cold Start and Minimum Viable Cohort

No evidence supports one universal minimum participant count.

| Proposed value | Smallest state that could produce the value | Cold-start dependency | What is currently unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal scarce-priority decision | One bounded team/process, a real decision, contributors and an empowered safe action owner | Trust, participation and action authority, not a global graph | Minimum response/issue diversity and whether a manager acts differently |
| Responsibility-aware routing | One verified owner/unit relationship and one routable issue | Accurate current responsibility plus a safe exception when the owner is implicated | Error rate, update cost and comparative lift over manual selection |
| Public participation | One defined open decision, declared participant body/method and accountable response | Legitimacy, reach and response—not population scale by default | Whether the intended job is participation, consultation or population inference |
| Cross-side contributor/action-owner effect | Enough qualified contributors to help an owner and enough responsive owners to make contributing worthwhile | Both sides must cross their mechanism-specific adoption path (E4–E5) | Side definitions, quality thresholds, reciprocal value and retention curves |
| Cross-organisation benchmark | A stable instrument, eligible comparable organisations and enough protected observations per declared peer stratum | Methodological comparability, contribution permission and disclosure control | Minimum cohort by stratum, opt-in bias, organisation identification and buyer decision use |
| Data-enabled learning | One repeated prediction/decision task with an outcome measure | Comparable labelled outcomes and a learning function (E6) | Whether more within/across-organisation data improves the task at all |

The current scarce-issue/vote product can deliver a bounded prioritisation cycle
without a global graph or cross-organisation participant base. That makes it a
valid non-network cold-start baseline rather than an incomplete version of an
assumed platform.

## Replicability, Portability and Multihoming

- Issue, vote, cohort, ownership and action-history primitives are readily
  understood and can exist in surveys, work trackers, HR/listening suites,
  product-discovery tools or public-participation systems. Novel combination or
  outcome remains possible, but primitive accumulation alone is weak exclusion.
- Explicit organisation relationships commonly originate in the customer or an
  existing directory/work system. Reusing them may improve integration value;
  it does not make the underlying facts exclusive.
- Data non-rivalry means another provider may learn from comparable data without
  removing this product's copy (E7). Privacy, permission and method may limit
  access, but those same limits also constrain cross-product compounding.
- Exportability, interoperability and parallel provider use can reduce lock-in
  (E9–E10). Actual replacement cost may still arise from trusted workflow,
  action history, integrations and organisational change, but none is measured.
- Buyers already can multihome across an HR suite, work tracker, survey tool and
  research provider. The engagement has no evidence that IssueNumber.one would
  become the system of record rather than another input or decision surface.
- Deliberately preventing export or relying on unsafe cross-context linkage
  would be a trust liability, not defensible proof of customer value.

## Contradictions and Disconfirming Evidence

- Relationship relevance to employee voice is not evidence that storing or
  traversing more relationships creates net value; the same context can chill
  participation, route to a retaliator or expose an author (E3).
- More benchmark contributors can reduce sampling variability while leaving or
  worsening selection/measurement bias; more slices and releases can increase
  disclosure risk (E3, E8).
- A public share can acquire attention without increasing utility for an
  existing user. That is distribution, not a direct network effect.
- Two named actor groups do not make a two-sided platform. Reciprocal
  participation must measurably change value (E4), and critical mass is not one
  generic count (E5).
- Shared infrastructure can reduce cost while remaining invisible to buyer
  outcomes and replicable by competitors; it must not be promoted into an asset
  claim without evidence.
- Data accumulation can create learning only when an observed task improves
  with additional data. Non-rivalry, portability and multihoming weaken an
  automatic exclusivity or switching-cost inference (E6–E10).

## Assumptions and Unknowns

- **Assumption:** “IssuerNumber.One” in the mandate refers to the repository's
  `IssueNumber.one`. If false, evidence routing changes.
- **Unknown:** The first decision user, urgent job and budget-backed outcome.
  Without these, “value” has no stable beneficiary or metric.
- **Unknown:** Whether relationship context changes a decision rather than only
  adding explanation or perceived sophistication.
- **Unknown:** Whether a contributor/action-owner reciprocal effect exists and
  whether either side retains because the other side grows.
- **Unknown:** Which data-enabled learning task exists, its outcome label and
  its within/across-organisation learning curves.
- **Unknown:** Benchmark instrument, eligible population, peer strata, minimum
  protected cohort and decision use.
- **Unknown:** Export, multihoming, replacement effort and actual switching
  events; no lock-in or switching-cost claim is supportable.
- **Unknown:** Whether action history is more valuable as customer-owned
  institutional memory than as a proprietary cross-customer graph.

## Confidence

**Low-medium.** Confidence is medium that the proposed mechanisms are distinct
and that no present artifact evidences a network effect, learning curve or
switching cost. Confidence is low on the ultimate user value and defensibility
because no IssueNumber.one customer, comparative outcome, cohort, retention or
switching evidence exists.

The most decision-sensitive evidence is a graph-versus-non-graph result for one
real decision, followed by repeat-use/retention and export/replacement evidence.
If no material outcome lift appears, H1 and H2 weaken sharply. If a permitted
context consistently improves the decision and the advantage strengthens with
additional eligible participation without increasing harm, the relevant narrow
mechanism—not the graph in general—would gain support.

## Risks, Dependencies and Recommended Research Priority

1. **Define one decision before naming an asset.** Specify beneficiary, action,
   baseline and outcome; otherwise graph density and issue count become vanity
   measures.
2. **Test one context field at a time.** A bundled “relationship-aware” concept
   cannot reveal which relationship adds value or harm.
3. **Require reciprocal value before network-effect language.** Measure both
   contributor and action-owner outcomes, repeat participation and retention.
4. **Gate benchmarking on method and disclosure.** Volume is not a substitute
   for eligible cohorts, stable instruments or safe release.
5. **Measure switching rather than engineering lock-in.** Preserve export and
   compare actual replacement effort, workflow loss and buyer retention reason.
6. **Keep H6 narrowed by default.** Cross-product transfer remains prohibited
   unless one named beneficiary/use passes the trust tests in the product report.

## Alternatives Kept Open

These are research alternatives, not recommendations:

- current scarce-issue/vote prioritisation with no graph claim;
- explicit fields and manual responsibility/cohort context;
- read-only reuse of verified Sneat Work relationships for one bounded purpose;
- issue/action history as customer-owned institutional memory;
- method-specific cross-organisation benchmark with isolated contributions;
- a contributor/action-owner workflow with no platform/network-effect claim;
- shared implementation infrastructure without shared derived signals;
- reject relationship enrichment, benchmarking or the entire intelligence
  direction if comparative value remains absent.

## Smallest Learning Action

Using only synthetic or already public material, define one internal decision
and one public decision with identical issues and scarce votes. Create a
non-graph version and a version containing exactly one necessary explicit
relationship context, then predeclare:

- correct or justified decision;
- time to route/interpret;
- identity guesses, unsafe routing or false-representativeness errors;
- what result supports, weakens or leaves H1 inconclusive.

This specification can be reviewed without collecting personal data. Execution
with target decision users remains a later experiment requiring sponsor
authorisation.

## Proposed Experiments

None executed or authorised in this iteration. Later candidates are:

1. the bounded H1 synthetic relationship-lift comparison above;
2. a synthetic H4 benchmark simulation that adds comparable, incompatible and
   systematically biased organisations while measuring accuracy, coverage and
   disclosure;
3. only after a reciprocal value proposition exists, a contributor/action-owner
   cohort test measuring value on both sides, repeat participation and retained
   paid use rather than invitations or signups.

Each requires a separate experiment contract with owner, consent/data boundary,
baseline, budget, duration and success/failure/inconclusive thresholds.

## Dissent and Conflicts Requiring Managing Partner Resolution

- The ecosystem principle that every capability should strengthen a shared
  relationship graph conflicts with the evidence that some signals have no safe
  cross-product use. H6's universal form is rejected; no canonical source has
  been changed.
- A relationship graph can be valuable infrastructure while failing to be the
  primary product asset or a defensible moat. Later synthesis must keep those
  levels separate.
- Benchmark coverage can improve with participation while validity or privacy
  worsens. H4 cannot be stated as a monotonic flywheel.
- Issue/action history may prove more decision-useful and less sensitive than a
  person-centred issue graph. That is a live alternative, not a conclusion.
- The absence of public evidence is not proof that no private demand or graph
  value exists; it is a prohibition on claiming that it has been demonstrated.
