# IssueNumber.one Independent Devil's Advocate Review

## Review Mandate and Independence

This review independently attempts to falsify the strongest forms of the
[business](business-strategy.md), [product](product-strategy.md) and
[go-to-market](go-to-market-strategy.md) recommendations. It did not author any
of them and does not offer a replacement recommendation.

The review used the accepted engagement brief, latest checkpoint, all six
research reports, all four experiment contracts and all three Draft
recommendations. No outreach, private data, experiment, campaign, architecture,
product build, canonical edit or external action was performed. No additional
public search was necessary: the engagement evidence is sufficient to expose
the decision-critical gaps. Public-source limits and failed searches remain as
reported in the owning research artifacts.

Evidence labels in this review mean:

- **Observed:** directly present in an engagement artifact or repository source.
- **Sourced:** asserted by a cited public or canonical source in an owning
  research artifact.
- **Inferred:** this review's reasoning from observed or sourced evidence.
- **Unknown:** not established by the current evidence.

Severity means:

- **Blocker:** the strongest recommendation or claimed learning decision is not
  supportable until the criterion is met or the claim is narrowed explicitly.
- **High:** the issue can materially change option order, buyer, product boundary
  or channel choice.
- **Medium:** the issue does not overturn the current restraint but can create a
  false positive or overclaim during execution.

## Executive Adversarial Verdict

The recommendations are strongest where they refuse the broad platform story:
they correctly reject present claims of a graph moat, benchmark network effect,
universal cross-product signal reuse, representative public opinion, absolute
anonymity and LinkedIn primacy.

They are weakest where they move from that restraint to a preferred direction.
Current evidence does not distinguish a Sneat Work capability from a standalone
scarce-priority tool, a facilitator-led decision service or no product. The
buyer-discovery portfolio favours the selected segment before evidence is
collected, the mechanism experiment bundles scarcity with the action loop, the
paid concierge test can validate consulting rather than software, and the
channel comparison cannot cleanly separate access warmth from channel effect.

Accordingly, the three recommendations should remain Draft. Their strong
negative conclusions survive. Their positive O2/Sneat Work ordering, commercial
unit and channel-learning claims remain provisional and currently
under-discriminated.

## Challenge Register

### DA-01 — O2's first-place rank may be ecosystem convenience, not strategy

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [business recommendation](business-strategy.md#recommendation)
  and [strategic resolution](../research/strategic-options.md#resolution) rank a
  Sneat Work priority-to-action capability above the standalone and no-build
  alternatives.
- **Strongest counter-case:** The ranking optimises for reuse, bounded context
  and ecosystem coherence because customer demand is absent. A standalone
  instrument or facilitator-led workflow can deliver the same scarce-priority
  and visible-action cycle without Sneat Work adoption, graph accuracy or brand
  dependence. Lower implementation friction for Sneat Co. is not buyer value.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** Every option has unknown buyer/problem evidence; O2's advantage
    is described as a relative hypothesis, not an observed outcome
    ([option table](../research/strategic-options.md#comparative-assessment)).
  - **Observed:** No supplied usage, repeat, payment, retention or graph lift
    exists ([current-state baseline](../research/current-state-and-demand-evidence.md#concise-answer)).
  - **Observed:** The graph report classifies shared infrastructure as a possible
    reuse benefit, not defensibility evidence
    ([mechanism taxonomy](../research/graph-value-and-defensibility.md#mechanism-taxonomy)).
  - **Unknown:** Whether any target buyer already uses Sneat Work or values a
    specific relationship enough to affect purchase or repeat behaviour.
- **Consequence if true:** O2 can consume ecosystem effort while losing to O1,
  a service-only offer or no build; the recommendation would mistake internal
  strategic fit for external demand.
- **Severity:** Blocker to the strong claim that the learning direction should
  be through Sneat Work; not a blocker to researching priority-to-action jobs.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Before O2 outranks O1, evidence must show at
  least two independently paid, repeated cycles in which an existing explicit
  Sneat Work relationship is necessary to the buyer-valued outcome and produces
  a recorded improvement over the same cycle with manual/standalone context.
  Otherwise the boundary must remain unresolved rather than scored in O2's
  favour.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** No current contract produces this comparison.

### DA-02 — Segment selection is built into the discovery sample

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [buyer/problem discovery contract](../experiments/buyer-problem-discovery.md#smallest-possible-implementation)
  assigns eight interviews to product, engineering, operations or departmental
  leaders and only two each to People/HR and public commissioners, then uses the
  comparator interviews to preserve or overturn the ordering.
- **Strongest counter-case:** The sample gives the preferred segment enough
  observations to meet a five-of-eight success rule while no comparator can
  meet an equivalent rule. “Clearly stronger” is undefined. The design can
  confirm the segment allocation chosen before the interviews, especially when
  founder access determines who is recruited.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** Public evidence identifies candidate paid jobs in employee
    listening, product discovery and governed public consultation but selects no
    initial buyer ([customer/market concise answer](../research/customer-market-evidence.md#concise-answer)).
  - **Observed:** The primary and comparator sample sizes and asymmetric
    decision rule are explicit in the discovery contract.
  - **Inferred:** Two comparator interviews can reveal a striking anecdote but
    cannot apply the same recurrence, buyer and budget test as eight primary
    interviews.
  - **Unknown:** Whether the initial segment wins under equal recruitment effort
    and equal evidence thresholds.
- **Consequence if true:** O3 or O4 could be ranked below O2 because they were
  under-sampled rather than because their jobs, buyers or safe learning paths
  are weaker.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Either test each candidate segment with the
  same eight qualifying recent-decision owners and the same five-of-eight job,
  consequence and buyer thresholds, or state that the two-person comparator
  interviews are contextual only and cannot support cross-segment ordering.
  Recruitment source, refusals and exclusions must be reported per segment.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** The current contract is asymmetric.

### DA-03 — No-build and standalone are named controls but are not operated as controls

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [business decision](business-strategy.md#decision-needed)
  says O0 and O1 remain controls, and the
  [strategic resolution](../research/strategic-options.md#resolution) calls O1
  the necessary product-boundary comparator.
- **Strongest counter-case:** O0 has no stop/park duration or opportunity-cost
  rule, while the experiment sequence is designed to advance O2. O1 has no
  distinct paid or repeat test separated from Sneat Work context. A named
  alternative that never receives the same outcome test is not a control.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** O0 is criticised for possibly becoming indefinite research,
    but no final timebox selects it
    ([O0 option](../research/strategic-options.md#o0--no-product-commitment-continue-research-only)).
  - **Observed:** The paid-cycle contract tests the recommended bounded cycle
    and defers the standalone-versus-Sneat Work choice until after success
    ([precommitted decisions](../experiments/paid-priority-cycle.md#precommitted-decisions)).
  - **Unknown:** Whether a paid buyer needs any Sneat Work context at all.
- **Consequence if true:** The portfolio can say controls were considered while
  only collecting evidence for the preferred branch.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Precommit the current three-week/25-hour
  discovery envelope as the O0 decision budget and a park interval if the
  permitted follow-up remains inconclusive. Before selecting O2 over O1, run at
  least one otherwise identical paid cycle with no Sneat Work relationship or
  product dependency and compare buyer outcome, delivery cost and repeat intent.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** Neither control has a complete decision branch.

### DA-04 — The mechanism test cannot separate scarcity from the action loop

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [relationship, scarcity and trust experiment](../experiments/relationship-scarcity-lift.md#smallest-possible-implementation)
  claims to distinguish whether scarcity helps, visible action helps,
  relationship context helps or the ordinary workflow suffices.
- **Strongest counter-case:** Variant B adds both scarce issues/support and
  explicit dispositions/terminal response to the manual baseline. Any B-over-A
  lift can come from scarcity, the action loop, clearer instructions or their
  interaction. There is no action-only or scarcity-only arm, so the experiment
  cannot produce the four-way learning it promises.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** A is ordinary submissions plus manual prioritisation; B adds
    scarcity and action together; C adds one relationship to B.
  - **Observed:** The recommendations themselves retain dissent that accountable
    action may be the real value while scarcity adds little
    ([business dissent](business-strategy.md#dissent-and-unresolved-challenges)).
  - **Sourced:** Both employee and public participation research treat
    follow-through as part of process quality
    ([product findings](../research/product-modes-and-trust.md#findings)).
  - **Unknown:** The independent and interaction effects of scarcity and action.
- **Consequence if true:** A positive experiment can preserve the scarce-vote
  thesis even when ordinary structured follow-through caused the result.
- **Severity:** Blocker to mechanism selection.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Use a factorial comparison with the same
  scenario and rubric: neither scarcity nor forced disposition; scarcity only;
  disposition/action loop only; and both. Add relationship context only after
  the winning non-relationship mechanism is known. No mechanism may be retained
  from a bundled lift alone.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** The current three variants cannot identify the
  scarcity effect.

### DA-05 — The relationship-context threshold can reward scenario construction

- **Target artifact/claim:** C succeeds if the relationship changes an
  otherwise ambiguous route for at least four of twelve decision owners without
  lower trust or more unsafe identity guesses
  ([metrics](../experiments/relationship-scarcity-lift.md#metrics-and-guardrails)).
- **Strongest counter-case:** The result depends on how many cases the author
  makes ambiguous and whether the “verified relationship” encodes the rubric's
  answer. Four corrected routes can be manufactured by scenario design rather
  than demonstrate transferable relationship value. The test also compares a
  structured field with a vague manual baseline, not necessarily a graph.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** The graph report says explicit fields/manual routing are the
    required non-graph comparator and no graph outcome exists
    ([candidate advantage tests](../research/graph-value-and-defensibility.md#candidate-advantage-tests)).
  - **Observed:** The experiment requires a predeclared rubric but does not
    require independent scenario review, a fixed number of ambiguous cases or a
    paired loss test.
  - **Unknown:** Whether the relationship adds information unavailable through
    an ordinary explicit owner field.
- **Consequence if true:** The experiment could validate “relationship-aware”
  rhetoric while proving only that showing the correct owner helps routing.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Before participant contact, an independent
  reviewer must certify that every participant receives at least one genuinely
  ambiguous routing case, that B and C differ by one explicit relationship
  field, and that the rubric is not inferable from wording. C passes only if at
  least four paired participants improve, none regress, and no additional unsafe
  identity/reuse inference occurs. The result must be labelled explicit-context
  lift, not graph lift.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** Current guardrails do not prevent an authored-answer
  effect.

### DA-06 — A decision-owner-only synthetic test cannot validate the two-actor trust journey

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [product journey](product-strategy.md#end-to-end-journey-and-observable-good-results)
  requires contributors and decision owners to understand visibility, null
  action and terminal states; the mechanism experiment recruits only decision
  owners.
- **Strongest counter-case:** A decision owner can route a synthetic issue
  quickly while contributors misunderstand identity, feel coerced, withhold
  minority evidence or never receive credible closure. The highest-consequence
  trust failure happens on the contributor side that the test omits.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Sourced:** Employment power and psychological safety change employee voice,
    and follow-up changes future signal quality
    ([product/trust evidence](../research/product-modes-and-trust.md#evidence)).
  - **Observed:** The synthetic experiment excludes contributors and real
    participant/owner state transitions.
  - **Observed:** The product recommendation's own Verifies section requires
    both actors and other-actor visibility
    ([Verifies](product-strategy.md#verifies)).
  - **Unknown:** Whether the promise is comprehensible and credible to the
    contributing actor.
- **Consequence if true:** A fast, apparently safe owner result can advance a
  product concept whose data quality and legitimacy fail at collection.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Before any trust or journey claim advances,
  run the concept with paired contributor and decision-owner roles under the
  approved non-sensitive scope. Both roles must correctly predict identity and
  content recipients, null action, permitted reuse and terminal outcome; every
  material item must receive a visible disposition; and there must be zero
  unsafe identity guesses. Decision-owner-only results cannot satisfy this gate.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** The current experiment tests only one actor.

### DA-07 — Paid concierge success can validate facilitation, not a product or commercial unit

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [business recommendation](business-strategy.md#recommendation)
  proposes selling a decision cycle, and the
  [paid-cycle experiment](../experiments/paid-priority-cycle.md#expected-learning)
  is expected to test payment, real decision value, repeat use and delivery cost
  before the smallest product implementation.
- **Strongest counter-case:** A skilled founder/facilitator may create value by
  framing the decision, moderating conflict and compelling a response. Customers
  may pay for consulting even if no software wedge, scarce-vote mechanism or
  repeatable product unit exists. A per-cycle commercial unit may remain a
  labour business with no software economics.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** The proposed implementation is explicitly manually facilitated
    and uses existing tools.
  - **Observed:** The experiment does not separate facilitation time from
    product/mechanism value, although it says the result may reveal which caused
    value.
  - **Observed:** No price is recommended and no delivery-cost baseline exists
    ([GTM commercial hypothesis](go-to-market-strategy.md#commercial-hypothesis)).
  - **Unknown:** Whether customers can reproduce the cycle without the founder
    and whether software reduces cost or improves outcome.
- **Consequence if true:** Payment and repeat can be misreported as product
  willingness to pay, leading to a build around a non-scalable service.
- **Severity:** Blocker to product commitment and commercial-unit validation;
  not a blocker to learning whether a service outcome is valuable.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Record facilitation and support time separately
  from tool use and report service and software value distinctly. Product
  willingness to pay is not established until at least two customers pay for a
  second cycle, complete it without founder facilitation, and retain the outcome
  at a price covering the full marginal delivery/support cost. Until then the
  evidence supports only a facilitated-service hypothesis.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** The current contract deliberately confounds service
  and product to minimise implementation, but its interpretation does not fully
  fence that confound.

### DA-08 — Payment and procurement branches are not precise enough to prevent overclaim

- **Target artifact/claim:** Paid-cycle success allows payment or an enforceable
  paid commitment; procurement delay is inconclusive; one additional cycle may
  follow an inconclusive result
  ([metrics and decisions](../experiments/paid-priority-cycle.md#metrics-and-guardrails)).
- **Strongest counter-case:** A founder can obtain a letter, purchase-order
  process or budget-owner statement without cash or delivery authority. Long
  procurement can preserve the thesis indefinitely as inconclusive, while
  small warm buyers who can pay quickly become the only apparent market.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** The contract distinguishes compliments from a commitment but
    does not define unconditional contract, collection deadline, cancellation
    rights or procurement evidence.
  - **Sourced:** B2B buying may involve several actors and consensus, but the
    engagement sources do not forecast this segment's procurement cycle
    ([growth evidence](../research/growth-and-commercial-evidence.md#evidence)).
  - **Unknown:** Actual approval actors, cycle length, contracting authority and
    payment timing.
- **Consequence if true:** Weak commercial signals can be upgraded to payment,
  or a poor segment can avoid failure through a perpetual procurement exception.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Before offers, define three mutually exclusive
  states: funds received; signed unconditional paid contract with price, scope
  and start date; and non-binding budget/procurement intent. Only the first two
  can count toward success, and neither counts as repeat until a second paid
  scope is accepted after the first outcome. Procurement beyond the experiment
  window is inconclusive once; if the one permitted follow-up still lacks an
  unconditional commitment, it becomes failure for this motion rather than an
  evergreen exception.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** Current wording leaves material interpretation to
  the operator after results are visible.

### DA-09 — Founder-led evidence is vulnerable to warmth, persuasion and survivorship bias

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [GTM recommendation](go-to-market-strategy.md#recommendation)
  begins with founder-led discovery and concierge selling; the channel test's
  direct route uses founder relationships or partner introductions.
- **Strongest counter-case:** Warm contacts can grant interviews, tolerate an
  immature offer and buy to support the founder. Failed or unreachable accounts
  disappear from a convenience sample. This can produce genuine payments that
  do not generalise to a repeatable buyer or route.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** The recommendation itself identifies founder selection bias as
    dissent ([GTM dissent](go-to-market-strategy.md#dissent-and-unresolved-challenges)).
  - **Observed:** The discovery contract does not specify a recruitment frame,
    consecutive sampling, relationship-strength label, refusal denominator or
    non-founder-access minimum.
  - **Unknown:** Whether the same problem and payment appear outside the
    founder's network.
- **Consequence if true:** The business can mistake founder goodwill for
  segment demand and underestimate sales effort.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Maintain the complete eligible-account frame,
  relationship source/strength, contact attempts, refusals and exclusions.
  Recruit consecutively against the eligibility rule rather than selecting for
  responsiveness. The segment claim cannot advance unless at least two
  successful problem/payment cases come from accounts with no prior founder or
  partner relationship; warm-only success must remain labelled founder-network
  feasibility.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** Bias is acknowledged but not operationally bounded.

### DA-10 — The LinkedIn comparison conflates channel, format and relationship warmth

- **Target artifact/claim:** The [initial-channel experiment](../experiments/initial-channel-comparison.md#smallest-possible-implementation)
  compares direct trusted access with “LinkedIn targeted, human-reviewed organic
  contact or a decision-useful post/landing path” and proposes a provisional
  route winner.
- **Strongest counter-case:** Direct introductions start warm; LinkedIn accounts
  may be cold. LinkedIn direct contact and one-to-many content are different
  mechanisms with different denominators and costs. Non-overlapping samples of
  twenty cannot attribute a difference to channel when warmth, format and
  account mix also change. The test excludes paid LinkedIn, so it cannot decide
  LinkedIn as a whole.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** Route B permits two materially different LinkedIn mechanisms.
  - **Observed:** The contract says accounts are assigned but does not state
    randomisation, blocking by warmth or matched eligibility.
  - **Sourced:** LinkedIn is instrumentable, but no IssueNumber.one economics
    exist ([growth concise answer](../research/growth-and-commercial-evidence.md#concise-answer)).
  - **Unknown:** Reachability, source warmth, organic distribution denominator
    and paid channel economics.
- **Consequence if true:** A route can “win” because its accounts were warmer or
  its format differed, and LinkedIn may be accepted or rejected for the wrong
  reason.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Fix one LinkedIn mechanism per comparison.
  Use accounts that satisfy the same eligibility rule, block or match on prior
  relationship/access strength, and randomly allocate where both routes are
  feasible. If equivalent allocation is impossible, report separate route
  feasibility and do not name a winner. Any conclusion must be limited to the
  tested organic mechanism; paid LinkedIn remains unknown.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** The current design cannot isolate a channel effect.

### DA-11 — Numeric samples and thresholds are decision-looking but uncalibrated

- **Target artifact/claim:** The four contracts use five of eight primary
  interviews, four of twelve relationship corrections, twenty-percent median
  speed lift, two of three paid organisations, one repeat within thirty days,
  one paid cycle per twenty channel accounts and a thirty-percent cost
  difference.
- **Strongest counter-case:** None of these thresholds is tied to a known
  baseline, expected variance, buyer economics, minimum meaningful consequence
  or the cost of false positive/false negative decisions. With these small
  samples, one participant or one warm payment can change the branch. Thresholds
  can create the appearance of precommitment without producing reliable
  discrimination.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** Each experiment disclaims population or market-size inference,
    yet its result controls strategic progression.
  - **Observed:** No threshold rationale, measurement reliability rule,
    sensitivity table or missing-data treatment appears in the contracts.
  - **Inferred:** A one-observation swing materially changes several outcomes;
    for example, one versus two paid organisations changes inconclusive to
    success.
  - **Unknown:** Natural variance and base rates for every metric.
- **Consequence if true:** Noise or one favourable relationship can trigger
  product/channel progression; one adverse case can kill a valuable job.
- **Severity:** Blocker to treating the portfolio as decision-ready.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Before execution, every numeric threshold must
  state its decision-cost rationale or be labelled exploratory. Each contract
  must include a sensitivity check showing the branch after any single
  participant/account changes state, plus missing/withdrawn-case handling. If a
  one-case change reverses success and no independent consequence-based reason
  justifies the cutoff, the precommitted result is inconclusive and requires the
  already bounded repeat rather than a strategic decision.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** Predefinition exists; calibration does not.

### DA-12 — The experiment portfolio cannot distinguish O2 from the full option set

- **Target artifact/claim:** The recommendations present a stage-gated evidence
  path for O2 after ranking O0–O6; the discovery experiment gives only brief
  comparator coverage, and later contracts test O2 mechanisms, payment and
  channels.
- **Strongest counter-case:** A successful O2 experiment shows that O2 may work;
  it does not show O2 is stronger than employee listening, public participation
  or another funded job. A failed O2 experiment also does not validate O1 or a
  comparator. The portfolio discriminates stages within O2 more than strategic
  options.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** O3 and O4 have sourced adjacent buyers/categories but no equal
    primary test ([strategic option set](../research/strategic-options.md#option-set)).
  - **Observed:** All contracts after discovery depend on the O2 direction.
  - **Unknown:** Relative demand, acquisition cost, trust-adjusted delivery cost
    and repeat value across options.
- **Consequence if true:** Later synthesis can describe O2 as “experimentally
  validated” and comparators as lower despite never testing them to the same
  bar.
- **Severity:** High.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Option-level conclusions must distinguish
  “failed,” “supported” and “not tested.” No comparator may be rejected or
  described as weaker on demand because an O2 test succeeded. Cross-option
  ordering requires the symmetric segment evidence in DA-02 or must remain
  explicitly a risk/reversibility ordering rather than a market ordering.
- **Status:** **Unresolved.** The current artifacts sometimes make the latter
  distinction, but the experiment portfolio does not enforce it.

### DA-13 — The highest-leverage existing evidence remains unobserved

- **Target artifact/claim:** Every recommendation sequences a sponsor-provided
  non-identifying demand inventory before external learning, but asks the
  sponsor to approve the O2 learning direction while the inventory remains
  unavailable.
- **Strongest counter-case:** Traffic, selected plans, qualified conversations
  or prior use could materially support O1, expose a different segment, or show
  that the old landing has not been distributed. Approving an ordering before
  reading the smallest available evidence can anchor later research on O2.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Observed:** The [checkpoint](../checkpoints/iteration-002-research.md#open-questions)
    identifies the inventory as the highest-leverage non-blocking question.
  - **Observed:** The current landing tests an older scarce-priority and suite
    proposition, so any result must be interpreted narrowly
    ([baseline findings](../research/current-state-and-demand-evidence.md#findings)).
  - **Unknown:** Whether any aggregate exists and what it shows.
- **Consequence if true:** The engagement can spend outreach budget to relearn
  what an existing aggregate already says or anchor on a direction contradicted
  by prior evidence.
- **Severity:** Medium.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Before authorising participant contact, the
  sponsor must either supply the approved non-identifying inventory or record
  explicitly that it is unavailable/declined. If supplied, option ordering and
  recruitment must be re-reviewed against it; if unavailable, “traction
  unknown” remains and no absence may be counted as zero demand.
- **Status:** **Partially resolved by sequence, unresolved in evidence.** The
  contracts place the inventory first, but no result or explicit decline exists.

### DA-14 — The broad trust-boundary and graph-reuse counter-case is already contained

- **Target artifact/claim:** The original broad platform hypothesis could have
  combined anonymous employee evidence, Moodometer, public research,
  benchmarks and relationship-graph reuse.
- **Strongest counter-case:** Cross-context reuse can re-identify contributors,
  chill employee voice, turn contextual inferences into durable person/team
  traits and make self-selected participation look representative.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Sourced:** The product/trust report documents pseudonymity,
    re-identification, employment-power, small-cell and contextual-integrity
    risks ([signal assessment](../research/product-modes-and-trust.md#candidate-signal-assessment)).
  - **Observed:** The [product recommendation](product-strategy.md#recommendation)
    explicitly excludes absolute anonymity, individual Moodometer joins, raw
    employee free text, benchmark claims and derived labels as shared graph
    signals.
  - **Observed:** H6's universal form is rejected in the
    [checkpoint](../checkpoints/iteration-002-research.md#decisions).
- **Consequence if true:** Without the exclusions, the product would have a
  potentially disqualifying trust and measurement failure.
- **Severity:** Blocker if the broad claim returns; otherwise resolved for the
  current Draft boundary.
- **Exact resolution criterion:** Keep every excluded signal and claim outside
  the initial promise unless a later artifact names a necessary beneficiary,
  purpose, permission, non-graph baseline, comprehension result, outcome and
  harm test. Shared infrastructure must not be treated as permission for shared
  derived data.
- **Status:** **Resolved in the current Draft recommendations.** Retain this as
  a regression condition.

## What Survives Adversarial Review

1. **Do not commit to the broad relationship-aware intelligence platform.** The
   evidence does not support a graph moat, benchmark flywheel, data network,
   public-opinion claim or employee-intelligence suite.
2. **H6's universal form remains rejected.** Several signal classes have no
   evidenced safe cross-product beneficiary; isolation is the defensible
   default.
3. **A bounded priority-to-action job is a legitimate learning candidate.** It
   can be explored without product construction, public-network cold start or
   sensitive employee data. It does not yet outrank O0/O1 on demand.
4. **Visible action and terminal state are necessary outcome candidates.** A
   ranking without acknowledgement, disposition or closure is incomplete for
   both internal and public modes. Whether this is differentiated value remains
   unproven.
5. **Trust boundaries belong in the product claim, not later architecture.**
   The current exclusions around anonymity, mood, free text, moderation,
   benchmarks and derived labels should remain.
6. **No product build is currently a valid experiment.** Manual and synthetic
   evidence can answer the next questions more cheaply and reversibly.
7. **Payment and repeat are stronger than waitlist or preference evidence.**
   They must be interpreted as service evidence until the facilitation confound
   is resolved.
8. **LinkedIn is an instrument, not the strategy.** The recommendation correctly
   refuses paid acquisition or platform primacy before a stable buyer and offer.
9. **Confidence should remain low.** The research is careful and source-linked,
   but customer, usage, repeat, payment and comparative mechanism evidence is
   still absent.

## Retained Dissent

- **RD-01:** O0 may be the strongest business decision, not merely the safest
  control, if the bounded discovery envelope fails or remains inconclusive.
- **RD-02:** O1, O2 and a facilitator-led service remain observationally
  equivalent on current evidence. Calling the direction a Sneat Work capability
  is premature.
- **RD-03:** The action loop may explain all future value while scarcity and
  relationship context contribute nothing or cause harm.
- **RD-04:** O3 and O4 are lower on trust/reversibility, not disproven on demand.
  The asymmetric discovery sample cannot settle their relative commercial
  strength.
- **RD-05:** Early payment may prove a consulting offer and founder trust rather
  than product willingness to pay or a scalable commercial unit.
- **RD-06:** The proposed LinkedIn comparison can establish route feasibility
  only after warmth and format are controlled; it cannot decide paid LinkedIn.
- **RD-07:** A bounded operational cycle can be safe while providing no evidence
  for anonymous employee listening, organisational health, public research,
  Moodometer combination, benchmarking or graph compounding. Success must not
  reopen those claims automatically.

## Assurance Disposition

The recommendations may proceed to Managing Partner resolution, but not to
In Review in their strongest current form. DA-01, DA-04, DA-07 and DA-11 are
blocking challenges. Every other unresolved High challenge must be resolved,
explicitly narrow the affected claim or remain as retained dissent linked from
the revised recommendation and later executive summary.

The broad-platform rejection and current trust exclusions pass adversarial
review. No promotion target is proposed.
